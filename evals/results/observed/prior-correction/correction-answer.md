## What changes

- **The speed comparison reverses for this reported session.** Cards' original 4 minutes excluded 6 minutes of setup; Notebook's 8 minutes already included setup. Comparable total-session times are **10 minutes for Cards and 8 for Notebook**. Notebook took 2 fewer minutes, calculated from those totals. Withdraw the original twice-as-fast claim for this total-session comparison. This does not prove Notebook is generally or causally faster. ([[Timing Correction]] S1; [[Timing Correction]] S3; raw/04 Timing Correction.md, lines 6–9; [[Definition Pilot]] S1)
- **The recommendation's rationale changes.** Cards-only study no longer has the apparent single-session time advantage. Retaining case-based work remains prudent given the accuracy-first priority and the audit's limited evidence on unfamiliar cases. Cards may still be considered for definition practice, but neither a combined program nor repeated-session time savings is established here. ([[Timing Correction]] S1–S4; [[Novel Case Audit]] S1–S4)
- **The newsletter loses its timing premise.** Its universal speed and complete-replacement promise was already unsupported by narrow evidence; the correction now explicitly withdraws the underlying twice-as-fast total-session comparison. The newsletter and correction share the pilot's origin, not additional independent experiments. ([[Team Newsletter]] S1–S2; [[Timing Correction]] S3; raw/04 Timing Correction.md, lines 3–4)

## What stays valid

- Exact-definition correctness remains **54/60 for each method**. Equal aggregate correctness in this session does not establish equivalence for all learning. ([[Timing Correction]] S2; [[Definition Pilot]] S1; [[Definition Pilot]] S3)
- The separate unfamiliar-case audit remains **Cards 16/24 versus Notebook 22/24**. Its self-selected sample and uncontrolled prior knowledge prevent a causal conclusion. It addresses a different task and sample, so it does not contradict equal definition scores. ([[Novel Case Audit]] S1–S2; [[Definition Pilot]] S1)
- The pilot's fixed order, repeated prompts, and lack of randomization remain limitations. The correction changes time accounting, not study design. ([[Definition Pilot]] S1; [[Timing Correction]] S1–S2)

## What remains unknown

Repeated-session setup amortization, unfamiliar-case timing, six-month retention, and wellbeing remain unmeasured. There is no supported estimate of longer-term time savings, no measured unfamiliar-case speed–accuracy tradeoff, and no basis for declaring a universal winner. ([[Timing Correction]] S4; [[Novel Case Audit]] S3; [[Definition Pilot]] S3)

**Revision condition:** reconsider replacement if a controlled comparison on relevant unfamiliar cases shows Cards preserves accuracy while reducing total study time, including setup across the intended number of sessions. This is a proposed decision test, not a result of the corpus.
