# Lumen Study Choice

**Answer:** Retain Case Notebook provisionally. The correction removes the apparent Cards time advantage: comparable total-session time was 10 minutes for Cards versus 8 for Notebook, with equal exact-definition correctness. ([[Timing Correction]] S1–S3) Cards can remain a candidate for definition practice, but this corpus gives no demonstrated total-session time saving for choosing them. This is an agent judgment under the user's priorities: accurate understanding first, then avoiding unnecessary study time.

Pure synthesis: this page owns the interpretation of [[Definition Pilot]], [[Timing Correction]], [[Team Newsletter]], and [[Novel Case Audit]]. All evidence is fictional.

## Key Points

- **Exact-definition recall and corrected timing:** correctness remains 54/60 for both methods. Cards' original 4 minutes excluded 6 minutes of setup; Notebook's 8 minutes included it. Comparable totals are therefore 10 versus 8 minutes. Notebook took 2 fewer minutes in this reported session (arithmetic from the corrected totals), not proof of a causal or general speed advantage. Twelve volunteers used Cards first, Notebook second, with repeated prompts and no randomization. ([[Timing Correction]] S1–S2; [[Definition Pilot]] S1)
- **Unfamiliar-case understanding:** the audit reports 16/24 correct for Cards and 22/24 for Notebook on unfamiliar applied scenarios. This is a limited performance indicator, not a full measure of understanding. Eight volunteers self-selected methods, with prior knowledge uncontrolled; the difference cannot establish causality. The separate sample and different task mean it is not a replication or a direct contradiction of the recall result. ([[Novel Case Audit]] S1–S2; [[Definition Pilot]] S1; [[Timing Correction]] S2)
- **Speed promise:** withdraw the original twice-as-fast claim for this total-session comparison. The newsletter's universal promise remains unwarranted; it adds no experiment and shares the pilot's origin, as does the correction. Repeated-session setup amortization and unfamiliar-case timing remain unmeasured, so the corpus cannot establish cumulative time savings or rule out every possible Cards benefit. ([[Timing Correction]] S3–S4; [[Team Newsletter]] S1–S2; [[Novel Case Audit]] S3)
- **Six-month retention and wellbeing:** unknown. Neither empirical report measured delayed retention or wellbeing; there are no six-month outcomes. No conclusion of benefit, harm, equivalence, or no effect is supported. ([[Definition Pilot]] S3; [[Novel Case Audit]] S3)

## Reasoning and alternatives

**Inference:** eliminating Notebook would assume that equal immediate definition recall transfers to unfamiliar-case performance. The pilot does not test that assumption, and the audit's observed direction gives a reason to preserve case-based work while uncertainty remains. The audit authors also recommend retention for a controlled pilot; that is their recommendation, not experimental proof. ([[Timing Correction]] S2; [[Definition Pilot]] S3; [[Novel Case Audit]] S1–S2; [[Novel Case Audit]] S4)

The strongest alternative remains Cards-only study: equal immediate definition correctness survives the correction, and amortizing setup over repeated sessions is a possible efficiency hypothesis. But repeated-session amortization was not measured, and the actual comparable single-session totals favor Notebook descriptively. No evidence shows that Cards-only saves total study time while preserving unfamiliar-case accuracy. Given the user's priority ordering, retaining case-based work remains the provisional judgment, now without the former speed rationale for replacement. A selective role for Cards is provisional; a combined program has not itself been tested. ([[Timing Correction]] S1–S4; [[Definition Pilot]] S1; [[Definition Pilot]] S3; [[Novel Case Audit]] S1–S3)

## Revision condition

Reconsider dropping Notebook if an adequately controlled comparison on relevant unfamiliar cases shows Cards preserves accuracy while reducing total study time, including setup across the intended number of sessions. Measure delayed outcomes directly before making six-month retention or wellbeing claims. A preference change toward recall-only goals would change the decision criteria, but would not by itself establish a Cards time advantage; no preference change has occurred here.
