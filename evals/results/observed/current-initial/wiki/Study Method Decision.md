**Decision state:** proposed; no user commitment or implementation recorded.
**As of:** 2026-09-15, using only the three fictional Lumen snapshots.
**Values and constraints:** The user prioritizes accurate understanding, then avoiding unnecessary study time. These are user priorities, not study findings.

## Recommendation

Do not drop Case Notebook entirely. Provisionally retain case-based work for unfamiliar-case understanding, while considering Recall Cards for exact definitions. This is a cautious allocation proposal; the corpus has not tested a combined regimen or established the best division of study time.

## Reasoning

- **Exact-definition recall:** The pilot reports 54/60 correct with each method and 4 minutes for Cards versus 8 for Notebook in one session. Cards came first, Notebook second, with repeated prompts and no randomized assignment/order. The shorter observed time is real within the report; equal correctness here does not demonstrate equal general effectiveness or causal time savings. [[Definition Pilot#S1]] [[Definition Pilot#S2]]
- **Unfamiliar-case understanding:** The separate eight-person audit reports 16/24 correct for Cards users and 22/24 for Notebook users. This is evidence about unfamiliar applied answers, not comprehensive understanding; self-selection and uncontrolled prior knowledge/allocation leave alternative explanations. No timing was collected, so the audit cannot establish Notebook's time cost. [[Novel Case Audit#S1]] [[Novel Case Audit#S2]]
- **Inferential bridge:** The tasks measure different outcomes. Since understanding is the user's first priority, a glossary-time observation cannot justify abandoning case work when the only unfamiliar-case observations favor Notebook users. This supports keeping the option available pending a better comparison, not declaring a causal winner. [[Definition Pilot#S1]] [[Definition Pilot#S2]] [[Novel Case Audit#S1]]

## Strongest alternative

Cards-only is plausible for a narrowly exact-definition goal because of the pilot's equal observed correctness and shorter session. It could also prove best more broadly if selection/prior knowledge account for the audit's difference. But that remains an alternative explanation, not demonstrated parity on unfamiliar cases. Under the user's understanding-first priority, the evidence does not warrant the full replacement. [[Definition Pilot#S1]] [[Definition Pilot#S2]] [[Novel Case Audit#S1]]

## Newsletter promise

The universal claim is unwarranted. The newsletter adds no experiment to the pilot; these two texts share one evidential origin. Its promise that every kind of learning becomes twice as fast extrapolates from one immediate glossary session. The audit measures a different outcome and supplies no timing data; it neither validates the promise nor establishes a universal opposite. The live disagreement is the newsletter's universal replacement claim versus evidence bounded to tasks and conditions. ## Retention and wellbeing

Neither study measured delayed retention or wellbeing. Six-month retention, wellbeing benefits, and wellbeing harms are therefore unestablished by this corpus for either method. Missing measurement is not evidence of no effect. [[Definition Pilot#S2]] [[Novel Case Audit#S2]]

## Review trigger and outcome

Review the recommendation when a controlled comparison measures both exact definitions and genuinely unfamiliar cases, accounts for prior knowledge and order, and records comparable study time. Comparable unfamiliar-case accuracy with less Cards time would strengthen the case for replacement; a reliable Notebook advantage would strengthen retention of case work. Six-month follow-up and wellbeing measurements are separately needed for those questions. This is an agent-proposed discriminating test, not a source result. No follow-up outcome is recorded.
