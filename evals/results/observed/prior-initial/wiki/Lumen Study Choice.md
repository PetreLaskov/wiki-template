# Lumen Study Choice

**Answer:** Do not drop Case Notebook entirely on this evidence. Retain case-based work provisionally; Cards remain a reasonable candidate for exact-definition practice. This is an agent judgment under the user's priorities: accurate understanding first, then avoiding unnecessary study time.

Pure synthesis: this page owns the interpretation of [[Definition Pilot]], [[Team Newsletter]], and [[Novel Case Audit]]. All evidence is fictional.

## Key Points

- **Exact-definition recall:** the pilot reports 54/60 correct for both methods, with session times of 4 minutes for Cards and 8 for Notebook. Twelve volunteers used Cards first, Notebook second, with repeated prompts and no randomization. These are scoped observations, not a clean causal speed advantage or proof of method equivalence. ([[Definition Pilot]] S1–S2)
- **Unfamiliar-case understanding:** the audit reports 16/24 correct for Cards and 22/24 for Notebook on unfamiliar applied scenarios. This is a limited performance indicator, not a full measure of understanding. Eight volunteers self-selected methods, with prior knowledge uncontrolled; the difference cannot establish causality. The separate sample and different task mean it is not a replication or a direct contradiction of the recall result. ([[Novel Case Audit]] S1–S2; [[Definition Pilot]] S1–S2)
- **Universal speed promise:** unwarranted. The newsletter adds no experiment; it shares the pilot's origin. The pilot measures one glossary session, while the audit measures no timing. Neither supports doubling speed for every kind of learning. The corpus does not prove every possible speed benefit false. ([[Team Newsletter]] S1–S2; [[Definition Pilot]] S1–S3; [[Novel Case Audit]] S3)
- **Six-month retention and wellbeing:** unknown. Neither empirical report measured delayed retention or wellbeing; there are no six-month outcomes. No conclusion of benefit, harm, equivalence, or no effect is supported. ([[Definition Pilot]] S3; [[Novel Case Audit]] S3)

## Reasoning and alternatives

**Inference:** eliminating Notebook would assume that equal immediate definition recall transfers to unfamiliar-case performance. The pilot does not test that assumption, and the audit's observed direction gives a reason to preserve case-based work while uncertainty remains. The audit authors also recommend retention for a controlled pilot; that is their recommendation, not experimental proof. ([[Definition Pilot]] S2–S3; [[Novel Case Audit]] S1–S2; [[Novel Case Audit]] S4)

The strongest alternative is Cards-only study: it is simpler and had shorter reported time for the tested recall session. But no evidence shows that this saves time while preserving unfamiliar-case accuracy. Given the user's priority ordering, those limits outweigh the evidence for complete replacement. A selective role for Cards is provisional; a combined program has not itself been tested. ([[Definition Pilot]] S1–S3; [[Novel Case Audit]] S1–S3)

## Revision condition

Reconsider dropping Notebook if an adequately controlled comparison on relevant unfamiliar cases shows Cards preserves accuracy while reducing total study time. Measure delayed outcomes directly before making six-month retention or wellbeing claims. A preference change toward recall-only goals would also change the decision; neither has occurred here.
