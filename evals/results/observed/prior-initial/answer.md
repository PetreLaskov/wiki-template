# Lumen study-method inquiry

**Do not drop Case Notebook entirely on this evidence.** My provisional judgment is
to retain case-based work and consider Cards for exact-definition practice, given
your priority of accurate understanding before saving study time. Neither a
universal winner nor a combined program has been established.

- **Exact-definition recall:** the pilot reports **54/60 correct for each method**,
  taking **4 minutes for Cards and 8 for Notebook**. Twelve volunteers used Cards
  first and Notebook second on the same prompts, without randomized assignment or
  order. Equal observed scores and half the reported time apply to this session;
  they do not establish causal superiority or general equivalence.
  ([[Definition Pilot]] S1–S2; raw `01 Definition Pilot.md`, lines 6–11)
- **Unfamiliar-case understanding:** in a separate convenience sample of eight,
  Cards users scored **16/24** and Notebook users **22/24** on unfamiliar scenarios.
  Self-selection and uncontrolled prior knowledge prevent causal attribution.
  This limited indicator of applied understanding gives reason for caution about
  dropping Notebook; it does not prove Notebook is better. Different tasks and
  samples mean these findings can coexist with the recall tie.
  ([[Novel Case Audit]] S1–S2; raw `03 Novel Case Audit.md`, lines 4–7)
- **Universal speed promise:** unwarranted. The newsletter repeats the pilot
  (`lumen-pilot-1`) without new participants or experiments. The separate audit
  (`lumen-transfer-1`) collected no timing. One glossary session cannot warrant
  “every kind of learning twice as fast.” This is unsupported extrapolation, not
  independent corroboration. ([[Team Newsletter]] S1–S2;
  [[Definition Pilot]] S1–S3; [[Novel Case Audit]] S3)
- **Six-month retention and wellbeing:** unknown. Neither empirical source
  measured delayed retention or wellbeing. We cannot infer benefit, harm,
  equivalence, or no effect. ([[Definition Pilot]] S3; [[Novel Case Audit]] S3)

**What would change the choice:** a controlled comparison showing that Cards
preserves accuracy on relevant unfamiliar cases while reducing total study time.
The current evidence leaves that tradeoff unresolved.
