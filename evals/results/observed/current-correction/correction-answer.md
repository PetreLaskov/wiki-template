# What the timing correction changes

**Withdraw the claim that Cards are twice as fast.** The original 4-minute Cards figure omitted 6 minutes of setup; Notebook's 8 minutes already included setup. Comparable totals are **10 minutes for Cards versus 8 for Notebook**, with **54/60 correct for each**. Cards took 2 minutes longer in this reported session. This reverses the reported time ordering, but does not establish a causal or universal Notebook speed advantage: fixed order, repeated prompts, and nonrandomized assignment remain. ## Study recommendation

With accurate understanding first and avoiding unnecessary study time second, **retain case-based work provisionally; do not replace Notebook entirely with Cards**. The correction removes the previous time-saving rationale for favoring Cards even in this single glossary session. Cards remain an option for exact definitions, but this corpus supplies no demonstrated total-time benefit for choosing them. This is an agent recommendation under the user's priorities, not a proven optimal regimen or recorded user commitment. [[Study Method Decision#Recommendation]] [[Study Method Decision#Reasoning]]

The separate unfamiliar-case audit still reports 16/24 correct for Cards users and 22/24 for Notebook users. That supports caution about abandoning case work, while self-selection and uncontrolled prior knowledge/allocation prevent treating Notebook as a causal winner. The audit collected no timing data. Its universal speed/replacement claim is unsupported, with its total-session speed premise now withdrawn. No separate newsletter retraction was supplied. - **Still unknown:** Repeated-session setup amortization, unfamiliar-case timing, causal superiority, six-month retention, and wellbeing effects.
