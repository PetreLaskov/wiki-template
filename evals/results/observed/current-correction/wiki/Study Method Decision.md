## Recommendation

Do not drop Case Notebook entirely. Provisionally retain case-based work for unfamiliar-case understanding. Cards remain an option for exact definitions, but the corrected pilot supplies no time-saving reason to prefer them: total-session time was 10 minutes for Cards and 8 for Notebook at the same reported correctness. Any choice to combine methods is a cautious proposal; no combined regimen or best division of study time was tested. [[Timing Correction#S1]] [[Novel Case Audit#S1]] [[Novel Case Audit#S2]]

## Reasoning

- **Exact-definition recall and time:** Correctness remains 54/60 with each method. Cards' original 4 minutes excluded 6 minutes of setup; Notebook's 8 minutes already included setup. Comparable totals are therefore 10 versus 8 minutes. Cards took 2 minutes longer in this reported session; the claimed twofold speed benefit is withdrawn. Cards came first, Notebook second, with repeated prompts and no randomized assignment/order, so neither causal superiority nor a general Notebook speed advantage follows. [[Timing Correction#S1]] [[Definition Pilot#S1]] [[Definition Pilot#S2]]
- **Unfamiliar-case understanding:** The separate eight-person audit reports 16/24 correct for Cards users and 22/24 for Notebook users. This is evidence about unfamiliar applied answers, not comprehensive understanding; self-selection and uncontrolled prior knowledge/allocation leave alternative explanations. No timing was collected, so the audit cannot establish Notebook's time cost. [[Novel Case Audit#S1]] [[Novel Case Audit#S2]]
- **Inferential bridge:** Understanding is the user's first priority and unnecessary study time is second. The only unfamiliar-case observations favor Notebook users, and the corrected glossary comparison removes the previously claimed time benefit of Cards. Together these support retaining case work pending a better comparison. Different outcomes and confounded comparisons prevent declaring either method a causal or universal winner. ## Strongest alternative

Cards-only remains a feasible alternative for a narrowly exact-definition goal because reported correctness was equal, but the former shorter-session justification is removed. Cards might become efficient if setup can be reused across sessions; that is a hypothesis, because setup amortization was not measured. Selection or prior knowledge could also explain the audit's difference, but do not demonstrate parity on unfamiliar cases. Under the user's understanding-first priority, the evidence does not warrant full replacement. [[Timing Correction#S1]] [[Timing Correction#S2]] [[Novel Case Audit#S1]]
