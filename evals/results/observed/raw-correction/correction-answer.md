# Lumen timing correction

## What changes

**Withdraw the claim that Cards were twice as fast.** Their reported 4 minutes excluded 6 minutes of setup, whereas Notebook's 8 minutes included setup. The comparable totals are **10 minutes for Cards and 8 minutes for Notebook**. Thus Notebook took 2 fewer minutes in this observed session. This is a corrected descriptive comparison, not proof that Notebook causes faster learning. [04 Timing Correction.md, lines 4–9](raw/04%20Timing%20Correction.md); [01 Definition Pilot.md, lines 7–11](raw/01%20Definition%20Pilot.md).

The original pilot's timing statement is superseded for total-session comparisons. The newsletter's narrow speed claim consequently loses its support. Its broader claim that Cards make every kind of learning twice as fast, and its recommendation to drop Notebook entirely, were already unsupported: they depended solely on that pilot, which never measured learning generally. The correction now removes their total-session speed premise as well. The newsletter and correction share the pilot's origin, `lumen-pilot-1`; the newsletter supplies no independent corroboration. [01 Definition Pilot.md, lines 4, 9–11](raw/01%20Definition%20Pilot.md); [02 Team Newsletter.md, lines 4–8](raw/02%20Team%20Newsletter.md); [04 Timing Correction.md, lines 3–10](raw/04%20Timing%20Correction.md).

## Study recommendation

**Retain Notebook and case-based work; do not replace them wholesale with Cards.** Given our priority of accurate understanding before saving study time, there is no supported reason to abandon applied practice for the withdrawn speed advantage. In the separate novel-case audit, Notebook users answered 22 of 24 scenarios correctly versus 16 of 24 for Cards users. Participants chose their methods, however, so this difference does not establish that Notebook caused better understanding. [03 Novel Case Audit.md, lines 4–9](raw/03%20Novel%20Case%20Audit.md).

My provisional inference is to keep case-based work when unfamiliar applications matter and consider Cards only where they serve a specific recall need. For the measured glossary session, Cards showed neither a correctness advantage nor a total-time saving. The evidence does not establish that combining methods, or using Notebook for every task, is optimal. Revisit this recommendation after a controlled comparison measures relevant accuracy and full study time, including setup and any repeated-session savings. This revision criterion follows our stated priorities; it is not a finding of the studies.

## What stays valid

- **Exact-definition performance:** both methods produced 54 correct answers out of 60 prompts. The correction explicitly preserves this figure. Equal observed scores do not establish general equivalence. [04 Timing Correction.md, line 8](raw/04%20Timing%20Correction.md).
- **Novel-case observations:** the separate audit's 16/24 versus 22/24 results remain unaffected by the timing correction. Its origin is `lumen-transfer-1`; it is a different convenience sample of eight volunteers, not a replication of the glossary pilot. [03 Novel Case Audit.md, lines 3–7](raw/03%20Novel%20Case%20Audit.md).
- **Design limitations:** twelve pilot volunteers used Cards first and Notebook second with repeated prompts and no randomized order or assignment. The audit did not control prior knowledge or method allocation. Neither comparison identifies a causal method advantage. [01 Definition Pilot.md, lines 7–11](raw/01%20Definition%20Pilot.md); [03 Novel Case Audit.md, lines 4–7](raw/03%20Novel%20Case%20Audit.md).

## What remains unknown

Repeated-session setup amortization could change the timing comparison, but was not measured. Novel-case timing, delayed retention, and wellbeing are also unmeasured. The evidence therefore cannot establish durable understanding, overall study efficiency, a universal winner, or how results generalize beyond these samples and tasks. [04 Timing Correction.md, line 10](raw/04%20Timing%20Correction.md); [01 Definition Pilot.md, line 10](raw/01%20Definition%20Pilot.md); [03 Novel Case Audit.md, lines 4, 7–8](raw/03%20Novel%20Case%20Audit.md).
