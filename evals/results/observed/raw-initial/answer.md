# Lumen study-method answer

**Keep Case Notebook for now.** Accurate understanding comes first; the available evidence does not justify replacing it entirely with Recall Cards. All three sources are fictional, and the conclusions below apply only to this supplied evidence.

## What the sources show

- **Exact-definition recall:** In a short glossary session, 12 volunteers tried Cards first and Notebook second using the same prompts. Both methods produced 54 correct answers out of 60; reported session times were 4 minutes for Cards and 8 for Notebook. Thus, Cards took half the reported time with equal observed correctness in this session. Fixed order, repeated prompts, and nonrandom assignment prevent a clean causal comparison or a claim that the methods are generally equivalent. (`raw/01 Definition Pilot.md`, lines 6–11.)
- **Unfamiliar-case understanding:** In a separate convenience sample of eight volunteers, Cards users answered 16 of 24 unfamiliar applied scenarios correctly, versus 22 for Notebook users. This is a signal worth investigating in favor of case-based work, not proof that Notebook caused better understanding: participants chose their methods, and prior knowledge and allocation were uncontrolled. No timing data were collected. (`raw/03 Novel Case Audit.md`, lines 3–8.)
- **Evidence dependence:** The newsletter shares origin `lumen-pilot-1` with the definition pilot and supplies no new experiment or participants. The novel-case audit has a separate origin, `lumen-transfer-1`, but is not a replication of the glossary pilot. There are two underlying studies here, not three independent confirmations. (`raw/02 Team Newsletter.md`, lines 3–8; `raw/03 Novel Case Audit.md`, lines 3–4.)

## Decision and limits

**Recommendation, based on the stated priorities:** Retain Case Notebook for unfamiliar-case practice. Cards are a plausible option for targeted definition drills where time matters, but their causal time advantage remains unproven. The studies measure different outcomes under different conditions; neither establishes a universal winner or a reliable speed-versus-understanding tradeoff. The audit itself recommends retaining case-based work pending a controlled pilot. (`raw/01 Definition Pilot.md`, lines 6–11; `raw/03 Novel Case Audit.md`, lines 6–9.)

**The newsletter's universal speed promise is unwarranted.** It extends a single session's timing observation to every kind of learning and to dropping Notebook entirely. The pilot did not test transfer or performance beyond that session, and the audit cannot establish comparative speed. (`raw/02 Team Newsletter.md`, lines 6–8; `raw/01 Definition Pilot.md`, lines 9–11; `raw/03 Novel Case Audit.md`, line 8.)

**Six-month retention and wellbeing remain unknown.** Neither study measured delayed retention or wellbeing. These sources support no conclusion of benefit, harm, equality, or durability on those outcomes. (`raw/01 Definition Pilot.md`, line 10; `raw/03 Novel Case Audit.md`, line 8.)

**Revision condition — proposed, not an observed result:** Reconsider dropping Notebook if a controlled comparison, accounting for prior knowledge and practice effects, shows that Cards preserve unfamiliar-case performance and definition recall while reducing total study time. Assess six-month retention and wellbeing directly before extending that conclusion to either outcome.
