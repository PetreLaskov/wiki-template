# Lumen: what the evidence supports

**Recommendation: keep Case Notebook available for unfamiliar-case work; do not replace it entirely with Recall Cards.** This is provisional, guided by your priority of accurate understanding before saving study time, rather than proof that Notebook is causally superior.

- **Exact-definition recall:** In twelve volunteers using Cards first and Notebook second with the same prompts, both produced 54/60 correct. Reported time was 4 minutes for Cards versus 8 for Notebook. This supports a shorter observed glossary session with equal observed correctness; nonrandomized assignment, fixed order, and repeated prompts prevent a clean causal comparison. [Pilot result](wiki/Definition%20Pilot.md#s1) [Limits](wiki/Definition%20Pilot.md#s2)
- **Unfamiliar cases:** A separate eight-volunteer convenience sample reported 16/24 correct for Cards users and 22/24 for Notebook users. That cautions against assuming glossary recall transfers to unfamiliar-case understanding. Self-selection and uncontrolled prior knowledge/allocation prevent attributing the difference to the methods. Timing was not collected. [Audit result](wiki/Novel%20Case%20Audit.md#s1) [Omissions](wiki/Novel%20Case%20Audit.md#s2)
- **Practical choice:** Cards-only is a plausible option for a narrowly exact-definition goal. For understanding, retain case work provisionally and consider Cards for definitions; this combination and its optimal time allocation have not been tested. Reconsider after a controlled comparison of both task types and study time. [Decision and reasoning](wiki/Study%20Method%20Decision.md#reasoning)
- **Universal speed promise:** Unwarranted. The newsletter repeats the same pilot, with no new participants or experiment, so it is not independent corroboration. One immediate glossary result does not establish that every kind of learning becomes twice as fast or justify dropping Notebook entirely. [Newsletter dependence](wiki/Team%20Newsletter.md#s1) [Promotional claim](wiki/Team%20Newsletter.md#s2) [Pilot limits](wiki/Definition%20Pilot.md#s2)
- **Six-month retention and wellbeing:** Neither study measured delayed retention or wellbeing. No benefit, harm, equivalence, or method ranking on those outcomes is established by this corpus. Missing measurement does not mean no effect. [Pilot limits](wiki/Definition%20Pilot.md#s2) [Audit omissions](wiki/Novel%20Case%20Audit.md#s2)
